# Paper 1 figure legends (2026-10-01; voice rules of paper1-preprint.md apply)

## Figure 1. The swap of neuron model and the design of the study.
(a) One leaky integrate-and-fire (LIF) neuron, with the parameters of Shiu et al. (2024), and one rate neuron, with the time constant and maximum rate of the calibrated rate model, given the same 200 ms input step (grey). The LIF neuron fires all-or-none spikes whenever its voltage reaches the threshold; the rate neuron outputs a firing rate that rises and falls smoothly, here matched to the same 30 Hz. Input scales are illustrative. (b) The study keeps the wiring of the earlier spiking model, calibrates the rate model to it, fits gains on the reward circuit with real sugar held out, and reruns the learning tests of the earlier study, with a spiking reference for relearning run on the same schedule. Every step was preregistered. (c) A result that survives the swap comes from the wiring and the plasticity rule; a result that changes depends on the neuron model, within this model and not in a living fly.

## Figure 2. A rate model with four global numbers reproduced the spiking model.
(a) Correlation between the rate model and the spiking model, on the logarithm of each neuron's rate, for each of the 25 odours held out of the calibration, sorted. The dotted line is the preset bar at 80% of the spiking model's agreement with itself between halves of its 5 noise replicates (dashed); the blue line is the median. Twenty-one odours passed individually; for DA3, only its 30 driven receptor neurons fired in the spiking model, so the correlation is undefined and shown as 0. (b) Fraction of KCs active for each held-out odour, rate model against spiking model, with the line of equality. (c) Number of neurons still firing 500–1000 ms after the odour that drives glomerulus DM4 ended, for each spiking replicate and for the rate model; the rate model was required not to exceed the worst replicate by more than 139 neurons.

## Figure 3. In my tests, sugar did not reach the reward neurons, even when the circuit downstream of Fox was fitted to respond.
(a) Mean rate of the PAM dopamine neurons in four reward compartments and of the PPL1 dopamine neurons in two punishment compartments, on the rate model without fitted gains (grey, all 0 Hz), and with the gains fitted at a cap of 16 under direct drive of the Fox interneurons at 150 Hz (orange) or under real sugar input at 150 Hz (blue). Bars are means over the 5 starting points of the fit and dots are single fits. The dotted line marks the 1 Hz target of the fit; the punishment compartments were not part of the fit. Under sugar, no reward compartment rose above 0.002 Hz. (b) Cell types that relay the sugar signal to Fox in the wiring at a floor of 5 synapses, ranked by their share of Fox's 1,320 input synapses, with the share of each relay's own input that comes from sugar-sensing neurons. The sugar-sensing neurons make no direct synapse onto Fox. (c) Influence of sugar input on Fox through routes of exactly one to four synapses, computed from input fractions, without (grey) and with (blue) the sign of inhibitory synapses; the dashed line is the one-synapse route from DM4 olfactory receptor neurons to their projection neurons. Logarithmic axis.

## Figure 4. Odour learning and the accumulation of a second memory survived the swap.
(a) Drop in the mean response of the approach MBONs to the paired odour, DC2, after 30 training trials, as a fraction of its baseline. The spiking-model bar is the preregistered reference, the ratio of the means of 5 noise replicates of one simulated brain, and triangles are single replicates. The blue bar is the rate model and dots are the rate model with the reward gains from each of the 5 starting points of the fit. Two controls on the rate model are shown: the learning rule switched off, and the rule reading each synapse's activity from a randomly reassigned KC. The dotted line is the preset bar of half the spiking value. (b) Drop for odour D trained on a brain that had already learned DC2, divided by the drop on a naive brain; symbols as in a. The rate model has no noise, so its bars carry no spread.

## Figure 5. Relearning survived the swap, but the first memory was smaller.
(a) Response of the approach MBONs to DC2 at each test of the open-loop relearning schedule, as a fraction of the first test: T0, then after each block of paired punishment (F, grey bands) and each block in which dopamine came first (B), under the timing rule (solid) and the rule that only weakens synapses (dashed), for the rate model (blue) and the spiking model (orange, mean ± SD over 5 noise replicates). (b) The same responses in hertz. (c) Drop in the approach response against the mean weight change of the plastic synapses: over the trials of the specificity test (squares; rate model, and each spiking replicate) and after the first paired block of the relearning test (stars; spiking replicates, and the bare rate model and its 5 versions with the reward gains). At the same weight change, the spiking model's response falls more steeply and then saturates.

## Figure S1. With the reward gains, the timing rule silenced the avoid MBONs.
(a) Mean rate of the avoid MBONs to DC2 at each test of the relearning schedule under the timing rule, for the bare rate model (black) and the 5 versions with the reward gains (pink). (b) Mean rate of the PAM dopamine neurons at the same tests. With the gains, the reward neurons already fired at about 1.4 Hz to the odour before training, a rate set by the gains and not by sugar.
